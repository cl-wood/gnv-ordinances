# ImagineGNV Comprehensive Plan: Housing Barriers and Recommended Improvements

This document identifies provisions in the ImagineGNV comprehensive plan that create barriers to housing construction and recommends changes to increase housing supply.

---

## 1. Special Use Permit Requirements for Height Increases

### Current Barrier
Multiple land use categories require a Special Use Permit (SUP) to exceed base height limits:
- Mixed-Use Low-Intensity (MUL): 5 stories base, up to 8 with SUP (line 1524-1527)
- Mixed-Use Medium-Intensity (MUM): 5 stories base, up to 8 with SUP (line 1590-1593)
- Urban Mixed-Use (UMU): 4-5 stories base, up to 6-7 with bonus (line 1641-1644)
- Office (O): 5 stories base, up to 8 with SUP (line 1759-1769)
- Commercial (C): 5 stories base, up to 8 with SUP (line 1799-1805)

Special Use Permits add time, cost, and uncertainty to the development process. They require discretionary approval, which can deter housing construction.

### Recommendation
- **Increase base height limits by-right** to 8 stories in MUL, MUM, and Office categories without requiring SUP
- Replace SUP requirements with **administrative review** for height bonuses tied to objective criteria (e.g., affordable housing inclusion, transit proximity)
- In the Urban Core (UC), increase the base height from 12 to 14 stories by-right

---

## 2. Low Density Maximums in Single-Family Category

### Current Barrier
The Single Family (SF) land use category allows only up to 12 units per acre (line 1336). This limits housing diversity in areas designated for single-family use.

### Recommendation
- Increase the maximum density to **18-20 units per acre** in SF zones to allow duplexes, triplexes, and small apartment buildings by-right
- Explicitly permit **"missing middle" housing types** (duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, cottage courts) in all SF zones without requiring rezoning

---

## 3. Minimum Density Requirements

### Current Barrier
Several land use categories have minimum density requirements (lines 1394, 1425, 1519-1522, 1582-1585, 1684, 1689-1690). While exemptions exist for small lots and mixed-use developments, minimum density requirements can:
- Prevent incremental development
- Increase financing complexity
- Discourage small-scale infill projects

### Recommendation
- **Eliminate minimum density requirements** in all categories, or
- Reduce minimums significantly and expand exemptions to lots up to 1 acre
- Allow developments to proceed at any density up to the maximum without penalty

---

## 4. Idylwild/Serenola Special Area Restrictions

### Current Barrier
Policy 1.5.8 (lines 2084-2103) severely restricts housing in the Idylwild/Serenola Special Area:
- 44 acres limited to only 88 residential units (2 units/acre)
- All units must be "single-family and detached"
- Adjacent properties limited to 2 du/acre increase above neighboring density

These restrictions lock in extremely low density in an area that could accommodate more housing.

### Recommendation
- Remove the **88-unit cap** and allow development consistent with underlying zoning
- Allow **attached housing types** (townhomes, duplexes) in addition to detached single-family
- Increase the adjacency step-up to **4-6 du/acre** above neighboring density
- Consider whether environmental protections can be achieved through setbacks and buffers rather than density limits

---

## 5. Adjacency/Transition Requirements

### Current Barrier
Policy 1.3.1 (lines 1956-1966) and Policy 1.5.8b (lines 2088-2103) require extensive buffering and transitional uses between high-intensity and low-intensity uses. While intended to protect neighborhoods, these requirements:
- Reduce developable area
- Limit density on sites adjacent to single-family zones
- Create de facto exclusionary zones around low-density areas

### Recommendation
- Reduce transition requirements to **setbacks and landscaping** rather than reduced density
- Allow full permitted density on all sites regardless of adjacent uses, with design standards for compatibility
- Remove the "2 du/acre increase" limit for adjacent properties

---

## 6. Residential Component Requirements in Mixed-Use Zones

### Current Barrier
Mixed-Use Low-Intensity (MUL) and Mixed-Use Medium-Intensity (MUM) categories require that at least 10% of floor area be residential for developments on sites 10 acres or larger (lines 1548-1561, 1615-1624). This requirement can:
- Complicate development financing
- Add construction complexity
- Deter commercial development that could later convert to mixed-use

### Recommendation
- Make the 10% residential requirement a **bonus/incentive** rather than a mandate
- Alternatively, allow developments to pay an **in-lieu fee** toward affordable housing instead of providing on-site residential

---

## 7. Inclusionary Zoning Threshold

### Current Barrier
New developments of 50 units or more must provide affordable housing (lines 776-778). This creates a cliff effect where developers may build 49 units to avoid the requirement, resulting in:
- Smaller buildings than sites can support
- Lost housing supply
- Fragmented development patterns

### Recommendation
- Lower the threshold to **10-20 units** so more projects contribute
- Implement a **graduated scale** (e.g., 5% at 10 units, 10% at 25 units, 15% at 50+ units)
- Provide meaningful offsets (fee waivers, additional height, reduced parking) to offset compliance costs

---

## 8. Parking Requirements

### Current Barrier
While Policy 2.1.4 (line 2171) mentions continuing to reduce parking requirements, the plan still:
- Regulates "location and amount of parking" (line 2007)
- Continues to "regulate subdivision of land, vehicle parking, and on-site traffic flow" (line 2011)
- Requires bicycle parking as part of development (line 3051)

Parking requirements increase construction costs, reduce buildable area, and discourage housing near transit.

### Recommendation
- **Eliminate all minimum parking requirements** citywide
- Allow the market to determine parking supply
- If minimums must remain, exempt developments within 1/4 mile of transit stops
- Set **parking maximums** rather than minimums, especially in transit-rich areas

---

## 9. Review and Permitting Process Complexity

### Current Barrier
The plan contains numerous references to review processes:
- Multiple approval stages (development orders, building permits, certificates of occupancy)
- Concurrency management system reviews (lines 2426-2434)
- Traffic impact analysis requirements (lines 2654-2656)
- Development plan review (line 2656)

Complex, multi-stage review processes add time and cost to housing development.

### Recommendation
- Consolidate reviews into a **single approval process** where possible
- Establish **by-right approval** for developments meeting objective standards
- Set **maximum review timeframes** (e.g., 30 days for by-right, 90 days for discretionary)
- Implement **parallel processing** of reviews rather than sequential
- Create a **small project exemption** for developments under 10 units

---

## 10. Concurrency Requirements

### Current Barrier
Policy 5.1.7-5.1.9 (lines 2426-2470) requires developers to demonstrate adequate infrastructure capacity before approval. While important for infrastructure planning, strict concurrency can:
- Block housing in areas with infrastructure deficits
- Shift infrastructure costs to individual developments
- Create uncertainty in the development process

### Recommendation
- Exempt **infill developments** from concurrency requirements
- Allow developments to proceed with **proportionate share contributions** rather than requiring infrastructure to be in place
- Establish a **concurrency bank** where developers can pay fees in lieu of infrastructure construction
- Prioritize public infrastructure investment in areas designated for growth

---

## 11. Tree Canopy and Environmental Requirements

### Current Barrier
Policy 1.5.3 (lines 2038-2048) requires tree surveys and cluster development in "Tree Canopy Areas." While environmental protection is important, these requirements can:
- Reduce buildable area
- Increase site development costs
- Limit housing density in desirable areas

### Recommendation
- Allow **in-lieu fees** for tree mitigation that fund tree planting elsewhere
- Permit **green roof and vertical landscaping** to count toward tree requirements
- Create **clear, predictable standards** rather than subjective review

---

## 12. Floor Area Ratio (FAR) Limits

### Current Barrier
Business Industrial (BI) category has a maximum FAR of 4.0 (line 1828). Other categories control intensity through height limits and build-to lines (lines 1759-1766, 1799-1805).

### Recommendation
- **Eliminate FAR limits** in mixed-use and commercial zones
- Control building mass through height limits and setbacks only
- Allow maximum utilization of permitted building envelopes

---

## 13. Missing "By-Right" Multi-Family in More Zones

### Current Barrier
Multi-family housing requires specific zoning designations (Residential Medium-Density, Residential High-Density, or mixed-use categories). Single Family (SF) and Residential Low-Density (RL) categories limit multi-family options.

### Recommendation
- Allow **small-scale multi-family (4-8 units)** by-right in RL zones
- Allow **medium-scale multi-family (up to 20 units)** by-right along transit corridors regardless of underlying zoning
- Permit **conversion of single-family homes to multi-family** without rezoning

---

## Summary of Key Recommendations

| Barrier | Recommendation |
|---------|----------------|
| SUP for height | By-right height increases with objective criteria |
| Low SF density | Allow 18-20 units/acre; permit missing middle housing |
| Minimum density | Eliminate or significantly reduce minimums |
| Idylwild/Serenola caps | Remove unit caps; allow attached housing |
| Adjacency transitions | Replace density limits with design standards |
| Mixed-use residential requirement | Make optional or allow in-lieu fees |
| IZ threshold at 50 units | Lower threshold with graduated scale |
| Parking minimums | Eliminate; set maximums instead |
| Complex review process | Consolidate; establish by-right approvals |
| Concurrency requirements | Exempt infill; allow proportionate share |
| Tree/environmental requirements | Allow in-lieu fees; clear standards |
| FAR limits | Eliminate in favor of height/setback controls |
| Limited multi-family zones | Expand by-right multi-family permissions |

---

## Conclusion

The ImagineGNV comprehensive plan contains language supportive of housing production, including Policy 1.1.1 which calls for identifying and removing barriers that "limit the construction of new housing" or "unnecessarily increase the cost of creating housing" (lines 825-828). The plan also acknowledges that "exclusionary zoning regulations like large lot sizes, wide setbacks, and low densities directly limit housing supply" (lines 730-732).

However, the specific land use categories and policies contain numerous provisions that contradict these goals. Implementing the recommendations above would better align the comprehensive plan with its stated objectives of ensuring "diverse and abundant housing" (line 796) for all Gainesville residents.
